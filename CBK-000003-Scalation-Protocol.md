# CBK-000003 — Scalation Protocol

**Project:** UK ChatGPT Community  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Stage 0 — Founder  
**Authority:** Foundational Engineering and Sustainability Document

> **Core Law — No Growth Without Capacity:** The community must not expand beyond its ability to govern, secure, maintain, document and responsibly operate what it has created.

## 1. Purpose

The Scalation Protocol defines how the UK ChatGPT Community grows safely and sustainably.

Its purpose is to prevent growth from becoming a source of:

- poor quality;
- security weakness;
- founder overload;
- financial instability;
- undocumented systems;
- governance failure;
- infrastructure failure;
- community fragmentation.

The protocol ensures that ambition is matched by real capacity.

## 2. What Scalation Means

Scalation is the controlled expansion of the community while preserving:

- quality;
- safety;
- sustainability;
- accessibility;
- governance;
- documentation;
- community trust.

Growth is not automatically progress.

Growth becomes progress when the organisation gains the capacity required to support it.

## 3. The Scalation Model

Every major initiative is evaluated across:

1. People;
2. Leadership;
3. Skills;
4. Infrastructure;
5. Finance;
6. Governance;
7. Security;
8. Documentation;
9. Community capacity;
10. Operational resilience.

## 4. Human Capacity

Before a major initiative begins, the project must identify:

- owner;
- maintainers;
- contributors;
- required specialist skills;
- expected workload;
- backup personnel;
- succession plan.

No critical function should depend permanently on one person.

## 5. Founder Capacity

During Stage 0 the founder may carry multiple responsibilities.

However, increasing workload must trigger delegation.

Warning signs include:

- important tasks repeatedly delayed;
- infrastructure becoming difficult to maintain;
- unanswered community requests;
- security tasks being postponed;
- documentation falling behind;
- excessive reliance on the founder.

When these conditions occur, recruitment or delegation becomes a priority.

## 6. Delegation Ladder

The community should progressively move through:

**Founder → Founding Builders → Working Groups → Maintainers → Community Leadership**

Responsibility should move toward people with the appropriate skills and reliability.

The objective is not to preserve founder control.

The objective is to preserve the mission.

## 7. Growth Stages

### Stage 0 — Founder

Typical condition:

One founder.

Primary focus:

- vision;
- documentation;
- initial GitHub;
- website foundation;
- governance foundation.

The organisation remains deliberately small.

### Stage 1 — Founding Builders

Approximate condition:

A small number of trusted contributors.

Primary focus:

- website;
- documentation;
- engineering;
- first meetups;
- community operations.

### Stage 2 — Working Groups

Approximate condition:

Multiple contributors with defined responsibilities.

Examples:

- Engineering;
- Community;
- Meetups;
- Research;
- Design;
- Infrastructure;
- Documentation.

Each group has a lead and documented responsibilities.

### Stage 3 — Regional Hubs

The community begins operating local groups in different cities.

Requirements include:

- local organisers;
- event procedures;
- safety procedures;
- communication channels;
- documentation;
- escalation contacts.

### Stage 4 — National Network

The organisation supports multiple hubs and larger programmes.

Requirements include:

- stronger governance;
- professional infrastructure;
- moderation;
- security;
- finance;
- formal operational procedures.

### Stage 5 — Strategic Ecosystem

Potential future condition:

Large-scale national or international participation.

Possible activities include:

- major research
