# CBK-000002 — Community Constitution

**Project:** UK ChatGPT Community  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Stage 0 — Founder  
**Authority:** Foundational Governance Document

> **Independence Notice:** The UK ChatGPT Community is an independent community. It is not operated by, owned by, or an official representative of OpenAI unless a formal relationship is established in the future. References to ChatGPT and OpenAI describe the technologies and ecosystem around which the community is organised.

## 1. Purpose

This Constitution establishes the principles and governance foundations of the UK ChatGPT Community.

It exists to protect the community's identity, quality, openness, safety, sustainability and ability to evolve.

## 2. Human-Centred Mission

The community exists to help people learn, meet, build, collaborate and contribute.

Technology is infrastructure.

People are the community.

## 3. Independence

The community remains independently governed unless its members formally establish another legal or organisational structure.

No person may represent the community as an official OpenAI organisation without formal authority to do so.

## 4. Respect

Every participant must treat other people with dignity and respect.

Harassment, threats, discrimination, intimidation and deliberate abuse of community systems are not permitted.

## 5. Openness

The community welcomes:

- beginners;
- students;
- researchers;
- engineers;
- founders;
- educators;
- companies;
- universities;
- public-sector participants;
- other people who share the community's principles.

Expertise is valuable.

So is curiosity.

## 6. Evidence

Important technical and organisational claims should be supported by evidence whenever reasonably possible.

The community should distinguish between:

- fact;
- interpretation;
- hypothesis;
- speculation;
- opinion.

## 7. Engineering Quality

Projects should aim for:

- clear documentation;
- maintainable architecture;
- testing;
- security;
- accessibility;
- reproducibility;
- transparent ownership.

A feature is not considered complete merely because it works once.

## 8. Responsible AI

The community supports responsible development and use of AI.

Projects should consider:

- safety;
- privacy;
- security;
- fairness;
- legal obligations;
- potential misuse;
- real-world consequences.

## 9. Community Before Popularity

The community will not optimise solely for member numbers, website traffic, social-media attention or GitHub stars.

Meaningful contribution is more important than superficial growth.

## 10. Physical Community

The community recognises real-world interaction as a central part of its identity.

Meetups, workshops, demonstrations, talks, social activities and local hubs should be encouraged.

The platform should help people meet rather than keeping the community permanently online.

## 11. Contribution

Members may contribute through:

- software;
- research;
- documentation;
- design;
- events;
- mentoring;
- moderation;
- education;
- project management;
- community support.

Not every valuable contribution requires programming.

## 12. Governance

Governance should progressively move from founder-led operation toward distributed responsibility.

The community should develop:

- maintainers;
- working groups;
- coordinators;
- reviewers;
- elected or otherwise accountable leadership structures where appropriate.
