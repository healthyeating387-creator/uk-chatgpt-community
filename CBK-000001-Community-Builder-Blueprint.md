# CBK-000001 — Community Builder Blueprint

**Project:** UK ChatGPT Community  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Stage 0 — Founder  
**Founder:** Julian Martinez Ramirez  
**Initial architecture and documentation:** Developed collaboratively by Julian Martinez Ramirez-Cruzado and ChatGPT (OpenAI)

> **Independent community:** The UK ChatGPT Community is an independent community. It does not represent, speak for, or claim to be operated or officially endorsed by OpenAI unless such a relationship is formally established in the future.

## 1. Purpose

The UK ChatGPT Community exists to create a place where people interested in ChatGPT, artificial intelligence, robotics, automation, research, engineering and related technologies can:

- learn;
- meet;
- build;
- collaborate;
- share projects;
- attend physical events;
- connect with universities and companies;
- discover opportunities;
- contribute to responsible technological development.

The community begins in the United Kingdom and is designed so that it can develop international connections over time.

## 2. Vision

> **Our vision is to help create one of the world's strongest AI collaboration ecosystems, where citizens, researchers, universities, companies, investors and public institutions work together to accelerate responsible innovation in AI, robotics and automation for the benefit of society.**

## 3. Motto

# Stronger, All Together.

## 4. The Starting Point

The project begins with one founder.

Stage 0 exists to establish the initial foundation:

- vision;
- documentation;
- GitHub;
- website;
- community principles;
- contribution processes;
- recruitment processes;
- physical meetup framework.

The founder is not expected to perform every future function.

The project must progressively attract capable people and delegate responsibility.

## 5. First Objective

The first objective is not maximum membership.

The first objective is to create a credible foundation that allows another capable person to understand:

1. what the community is;
2. why it exists;
3. how it works;
4. what is being built;
5. how they can contribute.

## 6. Four Stage-0 Pillars

### Vision

Define the purpose and long-term direction.

### Documentation

Create the permanent engineering and organisational memory.

### GitHub

Provide the engineering workspace and source of truth.

### Website

Provide the public home of the community.

## 7. Website

The website is the public front door of the community.

The initial platform should ultimately allow people to:

### Discover

- understand the community;
- read the vision;
- read the manifesto;
- understand DRAC;
- discover current initiatives.

### Meet

- find physical meetups;
- discover local hubs;
- register for events;
- connect with other members.

### Build

- discover GitHub projects;
- find projects seeking contributors;
- share completed work;
- follow project progress.

### Learn

- read guides;
- access tutorials;
- discover research;
- access educational resources;
- watch community talks.

### Connect

- discover members;
- find researchers;
- discover universities;
- discover companies;
- discover working groups.

### Participate

- become a member;
- become a contributor;
- become a Founding Builder;
- organise a meetup;
- propose a project;
- start or support a local hub.

### Governance

- read the Constitution;
- read community rules;
- understand leadership;
- view important decisions;
- understand transparency processes.

The first website will be deliberately smaller than this future architecture.

## 8. Physical Community

Physical interaction is a core part of the project.

The website must therefore be designed around real-world participation as well as online activity.

Possible activities include:

- AI build nights;
- robotics demonstrations;
- university events;
- research talks;
- AI and sport events;
- startup evenings;
- workshops;
- project demo days;
- company visits;
- community social events.

The guiding principle is:

> **Digital infrastructure connects the community. Physical experiences make the community real.**

## 9. GitHub

GitHub is the engineering workshop and source of truth for the technical project.

The initial repository is:

`uk-chatgpt-community`

Future repositories may be introduced when there is a clear need.

The project should avoid creating unnecessary repositories during Stage 0.

## 10. Founding Builder Programme

Once the initial foundation exists, the community will seek a small number of capable Founding Builders.

Possible initial positions include:

- Technical Lead;
- Web Builder;
- UX/UI Builder;
- Documentation Lead;
- Community Lead;
- Meetup Lead;
- Research Lead;
- Security Adviser;
- Infrastructure Adviser.

One person may initially cover multiple responsibilities.

Roles must eventually include:

- purpose;
- responsibilities;
- skills;
- expected commitment;
- access level;
- review process;
- succession plan.

## 11. Delegation Principle

The project should progressively move through:

**Founder → Builders → Working Groups → Community → Network**

The founder establishes the foundation.

Builders establish systems.

Leaders establish teams.

Teams establish hubs.

Hubs establish communities.

Communities create the wider ecosystem.

## 12. Access Principle

Administrative access is earned according to responsibility.

The initial model is:

**Public → Member → Contributor → Maintainer → Lead → Administrator**

No contributor should receive unrestricted administrative access simply because they have joined the project.

Critical infrastructure should never depend on a single person.

## 13. Scalation Protocol

Every major initiative must identify:

1. who is responsible;
2. what skills are required;
3. how much work is required;
4. what infrastructure is required;
5. what it costs;
6. how it will be maintained;
7. who can replace the responsible person;
8. what happens if participation increases rapidly;
9. what happens if participation falls;
10. when the initiative will be reviewed.

### Constitutional Law — No Growth Without Capacity

The community must not expand beyond its ability to govern, secure, maintain, document and responsibly operate what it has created.

## 14. Reality Check Panel

Before an important milestone is accepted, the project asks:

### RCP-01
Does it actually exist?

### RCP-02
Can another person understand it without the founder explaining it personally?

### RCP-03
Can another person operate it?

### RCP-04
Can another person maintain it?

### RCP-05
Is it sufficiently safe and controlled?

### RCP-06
Is the cost appropriate for the current stage?

### RCP-07
What happens if usage becomes ten times larger?

## 15. DRAC

DRAC is a long-term strategic programme concept of the UK ChatGPT Community.

**DRAC = Datacentres • Research • Automation • Community**

The programme explores how communities, universities, companies, researchers, investors and public institutions could collaborate around the development of AI infrastructure, research, automation and community capability.

DRAC is a future programme, not a claim that the community currently owns or operates datacentres or frontier AI infrastructure.

## 16. AI as National Infrastructure

The community recognises that advanced AI is becoming increasingly important to national economic, scientific and technological capability.

The community therefore supports informed collaboration among:

- citizens;
- researchers;
- universities;
- startups;
- companies;
- investors;
- engineers;
- public institutions;
- open-source communities.

The community does not claim authority over national policy or infrastructure decisions. Those responsibilities remain with the appropriate governments and institutions.

## 17. Independence

The UK ChatGPT Community is independent.

It is inspired by the impact of ChatGPT and OpenAI's work in artificial intelligence.

The community may seek future collaboration with OpenAI and other organisations, but no partnership, endorsement, representation or affiliation is assumed unless formally established.

## 18. Stage-0 Definition of Done

Stage 0 is complete when:

- the vision is documented;
- the Community Builder Kit exists;
- the GitHub foundation exists;
- the website exists;
- contribution rules exist;
- the recruitment process exists;
- the meetup framework exists;
- access and security principles exist;
- the Scalation Protocol has been applied;
- the Reality Check Panel has been applied;
- external people can understand the project without requiring the founder to explain everything personally.

At that point the community may evaluate whether it is ready for Stage 1.
