# CBK-000004 — Reality Check Panel

**Project:** UK ChatGPT Community  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Stage 0 — Founder  
**Authority:** Foundational Validation Document

## 1. Purpose

The Reality Check Panel (RCP) exists to prevent the community from confusing ambition with reality.

Its purpose is to determine whether an idea, project, feature, partnership or expansion:

- actually exists;
- is feasible;
- has sufficient evidence;
- has sufficient people;
- can be operated;
- can be maintained;
- is safe enough for its stage;
- is financially and technically reasonable.

The RCP does not exist to kill ambitious ideas.

It exists to help good ideas survive contact with reality.

## 2. Core Principle

> **An idea earns the right to continue through evidence, not enthusiasm.**

## 3. When RCP Is Required

RCP should be used for significant decisions involving:

- public launches;
- major website features;
- new repositories;
- production infrastructure;
- major partnerships;
- financial commitments;
- new community programmes;
- regional hubs;
- significant data collection;
- security-sensitive changes;
- major DRAC initiatives.

Small routine decisions do not require a full panel.

## 4. The Seven Reality Questions

### RCP-01 — Does It Exist?

Can we demonstrate that the proposed thing actually exists?

Evidence may include:

- working prototype;
- repository;
- documentation;
- live webpage;
- completed event;
- test results;
- signed agreement where appropriate.

If it does not exist, it remains a proposal.

### RCP-02 — Can Another Person Understand It?

Could a capable person who was not involved in creating it understand:

- what it is;
- why it exists;
- how it works;
- what is required;
- what remains incomplete?

If not, documentation must improve.

### RCP-03 — Can Another Person Operate It?

Can someone other than the founder perform the normal operational tasks?

If not, the project has excessive founder dependency.

### RCP-04 — Can Another Person Maintain It?

Can someone else fix problems, update it and continue development?

If not, maintenance risk is too high.

### RCP-05 — Is It Safe Enough?

Consider:

- security;
- privacy;
- legal requirements;
- access control;
- misuse;
- physical safety where relevant;
- reputational risk.

The level of review must match the consequences of failure.

### RCP-06 — Is It Affordable for Its Stage?

The proposal must identify:

- current cost;
- expected future cost;
- maintenance cost;
- funding source;
- fallback if funding disappears.

A project should not create permanent financial obligations casually.

### RCP-07 — Can It Survive Growth?

Apply the Scalation Protocol.

Ask:

> What happens if participation becomes ten times larger?

Also ask:

> What happens if participation is ten times smaller?

The system should remain viable in both scenarios or have a documented transition plan.

## 5. Evidence Classification

Evidence should be classified as:

**VERIFIED**

Directly demonstrated or supported by reliable evidence.

**SUPPORTED**

Strong evidence exists, but further verification is useful.

**PLAUSIBLE**

Reasonable hypothesis requiring testing.

**SPECULATIVE**

Interesting possibility without sufficient evidence.

**UNKNOWN**

The information required to decide is missing.

## 6. RCP Outcomes

### PASS

Evidence supports continuation.

### PASS WITH CONDITIONS

The project may continue after specified conditions are satisfied.

### RESEARCH REQUIRED

More evidence is needed.

### PROTOTYPE REQUIRED

The concept should be demonstrated before a larger commitment.

### DELEGATION REQUIRED

Appropriate expertise or leadership must be recruited first.

### HOLD

The project should wait.

### STOP

The evidence does not currently justify continuation.

A STOP decision does not permanently prohibit future reconsideration.

## 7. Human Resources Review

Every significant proposal must identify:

- responsible person;
- required expertise;
- estimated workload;
- backup person;
- reviewer;
- succession approach.

If the proposal requires expertise unavailable inside the community, recruitment or external advice should occur before implementation.

## 8. Technical Review

Where applicable, examine:

- architecture;
- dependencies;
- performance;
- reliability;
- security;
- testing;
- deployment;
- monitoring;
- backups;
- recovery.

## 9. Community Review

Consider:

- member benefit;
- accessibility;
- inclusiveness;
- moderation requirements;
- effect on meetup activity;
- volunteer workload;
- community trust.

## 10. Financial Review

Identify:

- one-time cost;
- recurring cost;
- expected growth cost;
- funding source;
- financial risks;
- exit cost.

No major financial commitment should be made without a documented sustainability assessment.

## 11. Partnership Review

For an external organisation, document:

- organisation;
- purpose;
- expected benefit;
- responsibilities;
- risks;
- conflicts of interest;
- independence implications;
- agreement status.

A hoped-for partnership must never be presented as an existing partnership.

## 12. Founder Dependency Test

Ask:

> If Julian became unavailable tomorrow, would this initiative continue?

If the answer is no:

1. identify the dependency;
2. document the process;
3. recruit or designate another responsible person;
4. create a handover path.

Founder dependency is acceptable during Stage 0 when documented as a temporary condition.

Permanent founder dependency is a scalability failure.

## 13. Ten-Minute Explanation Test

A new contributor should be able to understand the essential purpose and current status of an approved project within approximately ten minutes using its documentation.

If this is impossible, the documentation should be improved.

## 14. Pre-Launch Checklist

Before a major launch:

- [ ] Purpose documented.
- [ ] Owner identified.
- [ ] Maintainer identified.
- [ ] Dependencies documented.
- [ ] Security reviewed.
- [ ] Privacy reviewed where applicable.
- [ ] Tests completed where applicable.
- [ ] Deployment documented.
- [ ] Backup/recovery considered.
- [ ] Costs identified.
- [ ] Scalation reviewed.
- [ ] Community impact considered.
- [ ] Exit or rollback considered.
- [ ] Decision recorded.

## 16. Relationship With Scalation Protocol

The RCP and Scalation Protocol are complementary.

**Scalation Protocol asks:**

> Do we have enough capacity to grow?

**Reality Check Panel asks:**

> Does the proposed thing actually work and make sense?

Neither replaces the other.

## 17. Relationship With the Constitution

The Constitution establishes principles.

The Scalation Protocol controls growth.

The Reality Check Panel validates reality.

Together they form the Stage-0 control system.
